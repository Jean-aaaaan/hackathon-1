The Problem

Enterprise sales teams are flying blind at scale.

A 10-rep team managing 300 deals across 9-month sales cycles cannot give every deal the attention it needs. Deals go cold. Champions go dark. Close dates slip unnoticed. By the time anyone flags a problem, it's too late.

01

Champion attrition is invisible

The person driving your deal internally goes quiet for 3 weeks. No one notices until a competitor has already had two calls.

02

MEDDPICC gaps aren't tracked

Reps know the framework but applying it consistently across hundreds of deals is impossible manually. Economic buyer is missing on 60% of late-stage deals.

03

Follow-up timing is guesswork

The right email sent the day a champion returns from leave is 10× more effective than the same email sent a week late. Reps never know when that moment is.

Architecture

The 6-agent pipeline.

At 2am (or on-demand), Vantage runs a sequential 6-agent pipeline against every active deal. Each agent has one job. Outputs flow downstream — researcher feeds grounding, grounding feeds prioritiser, prioritiser feeds drafter. The result is a fully updated Account State Object (ASO) per deal, ready in the inbox by morning.

Voice fidelity

The Drafter writes in the rep's voice — not a generic AI tone. It is trained on the rep's actual sent emails (via Outlook or HubSpot) to extract their writing patterns: typical openers, CTA phrasing, sentence length, words they avoid. Every draft cites every fact it uses to a verified source.

Drafts are not sent automatically. They arrive in the Agent Inbox for rep review. Declined drafts with a reason category become training signals that improve future output.

Framework · MEDDPICC

MEDDPICC — applied to every deal, every night.

MEDDPICC is the gold-standard enterprise deal qualification framework. Vantage scores all 8 components on a 0–1 scale based on confirmed evidence from CRM data, emails, and call transcripts — not rep estimates. Economic Buyer and Champion are weighted 2× in the overall score because they are the two most predictive components of deal close.

M

Metrics

Quantified business impact agreed with the buyer. ROI, cost savings, revenue impact. 0 = no business case. 1 = fully agreed with EB sign-off.

Weight: 1×
E

Economic Buyer

The person who controls the budget and can say YES alone. Not just identified — engaged. 0.5 = identified, 1.0 = actively engaged in the deal.

Weight: 2× (critical)
D

Decision Criteria

The formal evaluation criteria the buyer will use to choose. Scorecard, RFP requirements, or stated success metrics. 0 = unknown.

Weight: 1×
D

Decision Process

Who approves, in what order, on what timeline. Full procurement and sign-off chain mapped. 0 = unknown. 1 = fully mapped with named owners.

Weight: 1×
P

Implicate Pain

The buyer's business pain quantified and linked to the cost of inaction. "Why change?" answered with evidence, not assumption.

Weight: 1×
I

Champion

An internal advocate who believes in you, has credibility, and sells internally on your behalf. Identified vs validated vs actively enabling.

Weight: 2× (critical)
C

Competition

Competitive landscape fully known and actively positioned against. Sole evaluation vs parallel POC vs competitor preferred by EB.

Weight: 1×
C

Paper Process

Legal, security review, procurement, and contract signing steps mapped with timelines. Often takes 30–90 days — not accounting for it is the #1 close date slip cause.

Weight: 1×
Framework · 3 Whys

The 3 Whys — urgency qualification.

MEDDPICC tells you if a deal is qualified. The 3 Whys tell you if it has natural urgency. All three must be present for a deal to close on its stated timeline without external pressure. If any is missing, there is a specific action the rep must take.

Why Change

Why leave the status quo?

Does the buyer have a compelling reason to change from how they do things today? Not "it would be nice to have" — a documented cost, failure, or risk that makes the status quo unsustainable.

"What happens to the business if they don't buy anything this year?"
Why Now

Why this quarter?

Is there a timing trigger that creates urgency now? Contract renewal, regulatory deadline, board mandate, budget cycle end, safety incident, or new leadership with a new mandate.

"What event or deadline makes this decision urgent in the next 90 days?"
Why Us

Why specifically your product?

Does the buyer understand your specific differentiation vs alternatives? Not just "we like you" — confirmed through a POC win criteria, a competitive displacement story, or an explicit statement of preference.

"What would they lose by choosing a competitor over you?"
Framework · 5 Risk Vectors

5 independent risk dimensions per deal.

Each risk vector is assessed independently on a four-level scale. A deal can have a healthy champion but a critical economic risk. Viewing them separately prevents a strong signal in one area masking a deal-killing gap in another.

🤝

Champion

Low — strong, active, enabling
Medium — engaged but unvalidated
High — dark 7+ days
Critical — departed or none
⚔️

Competition

Low — sole evaluation
Medium — mentioned, unclear
High — active parallel POC
Critical — competitor preferred by EB
📅

Timeline

Low — first date, MAP current
Medium — 1 slip or stale MAP
High — 2 slips or no MAP
Critical — 3+ slips, no plan
💰

Economic

Low — budget confirmed by EB
Medium — assumed, unconfirmed
High — EB not engaged at Proposal
Critical — freeze or eliminated
👥

Stakeholder

Low — 3+ contacts, EB engaged
Medium — 2 contacts, no EB
High — missing EB or technical buyer
Critical — single thread, >$100K

Framework · ICP Scoring

ICP fit scored on every deal automatically.

Every account is scored 0–1 for Ideal Customer Profile fit, with matching factors and gaps called out. Reps can filter and sort the inbox by ICP score. High urgency + low ICP = a deal that shouldn't be in the pipeline. High ICP + low urgency = a deal that needs a push.

Strong ICP signals (boost score)

Champion role matches: HSE Manager, HSQE Director, VP Safety
Industry match: construction, O&G, manufacturing, mining, logistics
Compliance mentions: OSHA, ISO 45001, Ministry of Labour, WSH
Multi-site operations with existing CCTV infrastructure
Recent safety incident or regulatory audit mentioned
500+ employees, government-linked or large enterprise

Anti-ICP signals (reduce score)

Small office or retail — no industrial operations
Pure software or tech company, no physical operations
No existing CCTV and no budget for cameras
Consumer-facing business (B2C)
Industry match but wrong buyer role (IT vs HSE)
Geography outside primary markets (MENA, SG, ANZ)
Quality Control

6 safety gates. Code-enforced.

These run in Python after every pipeline run, before any draft reaches the inbox. The model cannot override them. They exist because prompt instructions alone are insufficient for production-grade output quality.

✓

Hard cap at 2 drafts per account

No matter how many drafts the model returns, only the top 2 reach the inbox. Volume is noise. Quality is value.

drafter_result.drafts[:2]
✓

Zero verified signals = no outbound drafts

If the Grounding Agent found nothing verified, outbound emails are suppressed. Internal analysis types only. No hallucination risk.

has_verified_signals = len(grounding.verified) > 0
✓

No champion re-engagement without a champion

Champion MEDDPICC score must exceed 0.2 before a champion_reengagement draft is generated. You cannot re-engage someone you've never engaged.

champion_meddpicc >= 0.2
✓

No ROI email before first inbound reply

roi_business_case and executive_alignment drafts are blocked if zero inbound replies are recorded. Discovery precedes value justification.

total_emails_received > 0
✓

Confidence capped by health score

A draft's confidence score cannot exceed health_score + 0.25. A 0.06-health deal cannot produce a 0.90-confidence draft.

min(confidence, health_score + 0.25)
✓

Sender stripped from stakeholder list

Before drafting, the pipeline removes the sender's own name from the stakeholder list. Eliminates self-addressed email drafts.

filter(lambda s: s.name != sender_name)
