## The World Before

It's 7:45am. A sales rep at a 200-person SaaS company opens their laptop. They have 47 accounts to manage. They open Salesforce — stale notes from 3 weeks ago. They open Gong — 6 call transcripts they haven't listened to. They open LinkedIn — someone at Acme Corp just posted about a board review. They open their email — 4 unread threads from prospects. By 9am standup, they've processed maybe 3 accounts. The other 44 are coasting.

The manager opens their dashboard. 20 deals in "Close Won" stage in Salesforce. Seven of them have had no contact in 30 days. Two of those deals just went quiet because the champion changed roles. Nobody knows. The quarter is going to miss.

This is Tuesday morning for every enterprise sales team in the world. The tools exist. The data exists. Nobody has connected them into something that works while the rep sleeps.

---

## What's Broken

Three things:

1. **Fragmentation.** The account context lives in 5 different tools — Salesforce, Gong, email, LinkedIn, Slack — and nobody has assembled it into one picture. Reps spend 2 hours/day assembling context that should be instant.

2. **Latency.** By the time a rep notices that a champion went dark, the deal is already at risk. Signals exist. Nobody is watching them.

3. **The human limit.** A rep can deeply manage 8-10 accounts. They have 50. The other 40 are running on autopilot — which means they're stalling.

---

## What This Makes Possible

For the first time, every account gets a dedicated AI agent. The agent monitors it 24/7, assembles the full context from every data source, detects signals the rep would never have seen, and delivers completed work — drafted emails, meeting briefs, risk alerts — every morning before the rep opens their laptop.

A rep with 50 accounts works like they have 8. A manager with 20 deals can see which 3 need intervention today and why. A quarter that was going to miss closes instead.

## The Feeling

When a rep logs in at 8am and sees 10 accounts with completed work waiting for them — emails drafted, briefs ready, risks flagged — the feeling is: **"This is working for me while I sleep."** Not "I have a better tool." Relief. Like having a brilliant assistant who never clocks off.

When a manager opens Watchtower and sees exactly which 3 accounts need their attention today — not 20 amber deals, but 3 specific interventions with reasons — the feeling is: **confidence**. They walk into standup knowing.

---

## The Moat

Three layers:

1. **Episodic memory compounds.** The longer an account runs, the better the agent knows that specific customer's sales motion — what cadence works, which message types get responses, which objections mean "wait" vs "walk away." This is trained on that customer's own history. Churning means losing it.

2. **Acceptance signals train the agent.** Every "approve" or "decline with reason" is a training signal. After 30 days, the drafts are better. After 90 days, the agent knows the rep's voice. No competitor can replicate this without the same history.

3. **Gold Data Layer trust.** When every fact is cited to source with a confidence score and conflict resolution audit trail, procurement trusts it. This is what enterprise buyers need to sign off on AI in their sales process.
